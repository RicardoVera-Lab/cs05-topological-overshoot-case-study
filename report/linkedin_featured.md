# CS05 — LinkedIn / Featured Copy

## Short Featured description

I published **CS05 — Investigating a Post-Drive Topological Polarization Overshoot**, an independent AI-assisted scientific research case focused on hypothesis competition, preregistration, reproducible analytics, and evidence-gated stopping.

The project began from a published active-matter anomaly and narrowed the problem to three competing explanations. I preregistered and implemented the first discriminating Python analysis before access to the required real-data trajectories, froze the core pipeline with SHA-256 hashes, and validated the logic against adversarial synthetic cases.

The final outcome is deliberately conservative: **the physical mechanism remains unresolved because the required external raw trajectory data were not obtained. No mechanism claim and no novelty claim were made.**

The public repository includes the technical report, code, preregistration, synthetic validation, hashes, claim ledger, negative results, and limitations.

Repository: `https://github.com/RicardoVera-Lab/cs05-topological-overshoot-case-study`

## Suggested LinkedIn post

I just published the first complete case study from my independent AI-assisted research portfolio.

**CS05 — Investigating a Post-Drive Topological Polarization Overshoot**

The interesting part is not that the project produced a dramatic discovery. It did not.

A published active-liquid-crystal experiment reported a brief increase in topological polarization after the external drive was switched off. I used that anomaly as the starting point for a structured research process: prior-art review, competing hypotheses, preregistration, a frozen Python analysis pipeline, adversarial synthetic validation, and explicit decision gates.

The real-data experiment ultimately could not be executed because the required charge-resolved trajectories were not available at the evidentiary standard defined in advance.

So the project stops there.

**No physical mechanism claim. No novelty claim.**

For me, that is part of the value of the case: a research process should be able to stop when the evidence stops.

The repository is public and includes the technical report, code, preregistration, synthetic tests, hashes, claim ledger, negative results, and limitations.

`https://github.com/RicardoVera-Lab/cs05-topological-overshoot-case-study`

This is the first of three research cases I am building to demonstrate work at the intersection of **data analysis, decision intelligence, reproducible research, and AI-assisted investigation**.
